New, open box, used and refurbished: what each means

  • New: Check that the seller is an established retailer with the original manufacturer warranty, and that the listing is not a gray-market import.
  • Open box: Open box means returned or opened, usually in working order. Ask whether the original box, accessories and warranty are included.
  • Used: Prefer sellers with high feedback and a clear return policy. Ask for photos of the actual card and, for GPUs, whether it was used for mining.
  • Refurbished: Look at who refurbished it: manufacturer or eBay-certified programs carry a warranty, seller-refurbished items often do not.

Checklist before you buy a used RTX 5090

  1. Check the seller: feedback score, account history and a return policy you can actually use.
  2. Ask for photos of the actual card, including the serial number sticker. Stock photos alone are a warning sign.
  3. Ask whether it was used for mining, rendering or in a server. Heavy long-term use wears fans and thermal pads faster than light gaming.
  4. Ask whether the manufacturer warranty transfers to a new owner. This differs by brand, so confirm with the seller.
  5. The RTX 5090 uses the high-power 12V-2x6 connector. Check the plug and socket for discoloration or heat marks, and make sure the cable is fully seated when you install it.
  6. It is a very large, high-power card. Check that your power supply and case can take it before you order.
  7. Test it as soon as it arrives, during the return window. Run a stress test and watch temperatures.
  8. Be careful with prices far below the median in the table above, and with listings that say box only, for parts or untested.
